# ACSM Progression Models in Resistance Training

## Summary & Consensus
The ACSM position stand provides evidence-based models for progressive resistance exercise for novice, intermediate, and advanced lifters. Continued adaptation requires progressive overload—gradually increasing the stress placed on the musculoskeletal system over time.

## Key Quantitative Findings
- **Double Progression Model**: For hypertrophy and general fitness, fix the load and progress repetitions from the bottom to the top of a prescribed rep range (e.g. 8 to 12 reps). Once all sets reach the top target, increase load by 2–5% for upper body and 5–10% for lower body.
- **Novice Overload**: Responds robustly to linear progression; can add weight or reps almost weekly.
- **Intermediate Overload**: Requires periodized progression; rate of adaptation slows, necessitating systematic load/rep cycles.

## Practical Prescription Rules
1. **Double Progression Rule**: Do not increase weight until all prescribed sets can be executed at the upper target repetition count with correct form and prescribed RIR.
2. **Increment Sizing**: Micro-load isolations (+1kg to +2kg) and use standard increments for compounds (+2.5kg upper body, +5kg lower body).
3. **Deload Protocols**: Implement a planned deload every 4–8 weeks (or reactively upon 2 consecutive weeks of performance stagnation), reducing weekly volume by 30–50% while holding intensity relatively steady.

## Limitations & Caveats
- Progress is non-linear; adding weight every single week indefinitely is biologically impossible.

# Mifflin-St Jeor Predictive Equation for Resting Energy Expenditure

## Summary & Consensus
This study derived and cross-validated a mathematical equation to predict resting energy expenditure (REE/BMR) in healthy non-obese and obese adults, comparing against indirect calorimetry. Systematic reviews by the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics have identified Mifflin-St Jeor as the most reliable predictive equation for the general adult population.

## Key Quantitative Findings
The mathematical formulation for Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR):
- **Men**: `BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) - 5 × age (y) + 5`
- **Women**: `BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) - 5 × age (y) - 161`
- Predicted REE was within ±10% of measured REE for 82% of non-obese subjects and 70% of obese subjects.

## Practical Prescription Rules
1. **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**: Calculate baseline maintenance calories by multiplying BMR by an appropriate physical activity level (PAL) factor:
   - Sedentary: 1.2
   - Lightly active (1–3 training days): 1.375
   - Moderately active (3–5 training days): 1.55
   - Very active (6–7 training days): 1.725
2. **Deficit / Surplus Sizing**:
   - Fat loss: subtract 300–500 kcal/day (safe rate of ~0.5–1.0% body weight loss per week).
   - Muscle gain: add 200–400 kcal/day (conservative surplus minimizing adipose accrual).

## Limitations & Caveats
- All predictive equations are population-level statistical models with an inherent ±10–15% variance.
- Calculated targets must be treated as initial estimates and adjusted based on observed 2–4 week body weight trends.

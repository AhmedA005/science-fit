# Loading Spectrum and Repetition Ranges for Hypertrophy

## Summary & Consensus
Muscular hypertrophy can be attained across a wide spectrum of repetition zones (from 6 to 30+ repetitions per set), provided that sets are performed with high effort and taken reasonably close to concentric failure (1–3 RIR).

## Key Quantitative Findings
- **Heavy loads (1–5 reps, >85% 1RM)**: Superior for maximal strength development; effective for hypertrophy but time-inefficient and causes high joint stress.
- **Moderate loads (6–15 reps, 60–80% 1RM)**: Highly time-efficient, optimizes the balance between mechanical tension and metabolic stress with manageable systemic fatigue.
- **Light loads (15–30+ reps, 30–60% 1RM)**: Produces comparable hypertrophy to heavy/moderate loads only when taken very close to true muscular failure (0–1 RIR). Causes higher cardiovascular and psychological fatigue.

## Practical Prescription Rules
1. **Primary Rep Range**: Anchor the majority (~70%) of hypertrophy training in the 6–12 rep range for compound movements and 8–15 rep range for isolation movements.
2. **Heavy Compounds**: Use lower rep ranges (5–8 reps) for heavy multi-joint compound exercises (e.g. Squat, Deadlift, Bench Press) to minimize cardiovascular limitation.
3. **Light Isolations**: Reserve higher rep ranges (12–20+ reps) for single-joint isolation exercises and machine work to spare joints.

## Limitations & Caveats
- Light-load sets (>20 reps) carried far from failure (e.g. 4+ RIR) produce substantially inferior muscle growth compared to moderate loads at the same RIR.

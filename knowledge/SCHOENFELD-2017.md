# Dose-Response Relationship Between Weekly Resistance Training Volume and Hypertrophy

## Summary & Consensus
This meta-analysis investigated whether higher weekly resistance training volume produces greater increases in muscle mass. The consensus confirms a graded dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and muscle hypertrophy in both trained and untrained individuals.

## Key Quantitative Findings
- **<5 weekly sets per muscle**: Resulted in minimal to modest hypertrophy gains.
- **5–9 weekly sets per muscle**: Produced significantly greater hypertrophy (+6.6% increase in muscle size).
- **10+ weekly sets per muscle**: Produced the greatest hypertrophic response (+9.8% increase in muscle size).
- Each additional set per muscle group per week was associated with an incremental increase in effect size of approximately 0.023.

## Practical Prescription Rules
1. **Minimum Effective Volume (MEV)**: Aim for a minimum of 8–10 working sets per muscle group per week to maximize hypertrophy.
2. **Weekly Allocation**: Distribute weekly sets across 2 or more sessions when weekly volume exceeds 8–10 sets per muscle to avoid intra-session diminishing returns.
3. **Upper Ceiling**: Higher volumes (12–20 sets) are beneficial, but diminishing returns occur past 15–20 sets per muscle per week for most individuals.

## Limitations & Caveats
- Individual response variation is substantial; genetic factors and recovery capacity influence the optimal threshold.
- Most included studies evaluated training taken close to failure (RIR 0–2).
- Count only hard working sets (RIR <= 3); warm-up sets do not contribute toward this volume threshold.

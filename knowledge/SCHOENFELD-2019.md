# Effects of Resistance Training Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy

## Summary & Consensus
This meta-analysis investigated the effect of training frequency (1, 2, 3, or 4+ times per week per muscle group) on muscle hypertrophy when total weekly training volume is equated versus when it is not equated.

## Key Quantitative Findings
- **When weekly volume is equated**: Training frequency has no statistically significant effect on muscle growth. Training a muscle 1x per week produces similar hypertrophy to 2x or 3x per week provided total weekly sets are identical.
- **When weekly volume is higher with higher frequency**: Higher frequencies lead to superior hypertrophy simply because they facilitate accumulating higher weekly volume without excessive per-session fatigue.

## Practical Prescription Rules
1. **Volume Equated Rule**: Select training frequency based on personal schedule, recovery, and time preference.
2. **Frequency as Volume Enabler**: When weekly sets for a muscle exceed 8–10 sets, divide the sets across 2 or more sessions per week (e.g., 12 sets/week split into 2 sessions of 6 sets rather than one grueling 12-set session).
3. **Session Efficiency**: Caps at roughly 8–10 sets per muscle per session to prevent junk volume.

## Limitations & Caveats
- Extremely high frequencies (5+ days per week per muscle) may increase joint and connective tissue irritation if recovery is compromised.
- Split routines (Upper/Lower, PPL) are primarily organizational tools to distribute volume cleanly across the week.

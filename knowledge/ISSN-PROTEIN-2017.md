# ISSN Position Stand: Protein and Exercise

## Summary & Consensus
The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) provides an objective position stand regarding protein intake for active individuals, athletic populations, and body composition optimization.

## Key Quantitative Findings
- An overall daily protein intake in the range of **1.4 to 2.0 g/kg/day** is sufficient for most exercising individuals to maintain positive nitrogen balance.
- **Hypocaloric & Fat Loss Contexts**: Higher protein intakes of **2.3 to 3.1 g/kg/day** (of fat-free mass) may be required to maximize retention of lean body mass in resistance-trained subjects during hypocaloric periods.
- Doses of 20–40 g of high-quality protein per meal maximally stimulate muscle protein synthesis (MPS).

## Practical Prescription Rules
1. **Caloric Deficit Baseline**: Set protein intake to 2.0–2.4 g/kg/day when in a fat loss deficit to counteract elevated muscle protein breakdown.
2. **Per-Meal Dosing**: Encourage meals containing 0.25–0.40 g of protein per kg of body weight (typically 25–45 g per meal).
3. **Safety Profile**: High protein diets (>2.0 g/kg/day) in healthy, exercising individuals show no detrimental effects on kidney function, liver function, or bone health.

## Limitations & Caveats
- Quality matters: Complete proteins containing adequate leucine (~2–3 g per meal) stimulate MPS most efficiently.

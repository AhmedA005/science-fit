# Protein Intake Dose-Response for Resistance Training

## Summary & Consensus
This landmark systematic review and meta-analysis of 49 studies with 1,863 participants evaluated the impact of dietary protein intake on resistance training-induced increases in muscle mass and strength.

## Key Quantitative Findings
- Dietary protein supplementation significantly increases fat-free mass (FFM) and 1RM strength during resistance training.
- **The 1.6 g/kg Breakpoint**: Protein intakes beyond 1.62 g/kg/day (95% CI: 1.03 to 2.20 g/kg/day) showed no statistically significant further contribution to gains in fat-free mass.
- The 95% confidence interval upper bound reaches 2.2 g/kg/day, suggesting that aiming up to 2.0–2.2 g/kg/day ensures capturing virtually all potential responders.

## Practical Prescription Rules
1. **Target Intake**: Prescribe 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg/day of total daily protein for individuals engaged in resistance training with a hypertrophy goal.
2. **Distribution**: Distribute daily protein intake across 3 to 5 meals throughout the day (0.4–0.55 g/kg per meal) to optimize muscle protein synthesis (MPS) stimulation.
3. **Caloric Surplus / Maintenance**: 1.6–1.8 g/kg/day is generally sufficient during energy balance or a caloric surplus.

## Limitations & Caveats
- Higher protein intakes (>2.0 g/kg/day) provide greater satiety and muscle preservation during hypocaloric (fat loss) dieting phases, which is why dieting targets are typically adjusted upwards.
